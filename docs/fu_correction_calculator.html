Five reasons fu corrections fail

1. Double-counting or omitting fu,mic. Divide scaled CLint by fu,mic once, then apply fub in the well-stirred model. Applying plasma binding twice — or forgetting fu,mic — is the most common error. Obach 1999

2. Predicting fu,mic from logP/logD outside the model's range. The Austin model mis-predicts strongly bound bases and out-of-space compounds; CLint,u scales inversely with fu,mic, so a several-fold fu,mic error becomes a several-fold CL error. Austin 2002

3. Non-linear / concentration-dependent binding. fu,mic is only constant when [S] ≪ Km and protein is in the validated range; at high substrate or protein loads the single-value correction breaks. Austin 2002 · Hallifax

4. Wrong reference fluid. Mixing fu,plasma with a blood-based clearance corrupts fub = fu,p/(B:P) — a large systematic error for red-cell partitioners. Rowland & Tozer

5. Correcting binding while missing the real cause. If the true gap is transporter/AO/UGT/biliary clearance or an in-vitro artefact, a perfect fu,mic correction just makes an incomplete model look precise. Poulin 2024
